Abstract

Physical reasoning is central to agents that step into the physical world. Models now excel at code and math, but their physical reasoning capabilities remain largely untested. Existing physical benchmarks evaluate goal-state matching rather than functional performance. This position paper argues that evaluating physical reasoning in LLM agents requires construction benchmarks, where physics verifies functional outcomes. To operationalize physical reasoning evaluation, we identify five capabilities spanning the full agentic loop from goal to verified artifact. Our audit of eleven physical benchmarks finds that the only two combining realistic physics with functional verification, the minimum requirements for testing physical reasoning, are construction benchmarks. Neither covers all five capabilities, yet both already show current models failing as task complexity increases. Progress depends on the breadth of benchmarks. We call on academia, game studios, and industry labs to expand construction benchmarks along both axes, widening capability coverage and deepening physical challenges. Their breadth determines how fully agents move from code and math to reshaping the physical world.

The verification problem

Current physical benchmarks evaluate goal-state matching rather than functional outcomes, the equivalent of checking syntax instead of running code.

The safety problem

Grounding LLM agents in the physical world makes physical reasoning a safety problem first.

Goal-state matching also credits physical hallucinations. The community might then optimize toward the behavior a benchmark credits.

The five capabilities of a physical reasoning benchmark

We propose two minimum requirements for evaluating physical reasoning under intervention with irreversible consequences: realistic physics governing every action and functional verification judging the outcome. Evaluation therefore follows an agent through the full loop, from a goal to that outcome. We decompose this evaluation into five capabilities, from goal-driven design to closed-loop refinement:

Generating a solution from a functional specification rather than following given step-by-step instructions.

Generating action sequences that obey physical constraints, anticipating how each action affects future states.

Acting in an environment with realistic physics, including gravity, friction, and collision, while perceiving the environment state to inform actions.

Evaluating whether an artifact achieves its intended function.

Receiving evaluation signals, detecting when goals are not achieved, and adapting upstream decisions accordingly.
